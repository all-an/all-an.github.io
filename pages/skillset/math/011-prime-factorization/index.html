Prime factorization

Arithmetic

The idea

A prime number p > 1 has no positive divisors other than 1 and p. Every integer n > 1 factors uniquely as a product of primes.

Key properties & formulas

The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (proved in Euclid's Elements Book IX and formalized by Gauss) states that prime factorization is unique up to the order of the factors.

n = p₁^a₁ × p₂^a₂ × ⋯ × pₖ^aₖ

Historical & theoretical context: Fundamental theorem of arithmetic. Core concepts include primes, factor tree, unique factorization.
